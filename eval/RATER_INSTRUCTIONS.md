# Rating instructions

Thank you for helping evaluate Kàwé, a tool that summarizes documents and reads the summaries aloud in Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa and English. This takes about 25–30 minutes. Please use headphones in a quiet place.

## 1. Audio clips (`audio_ratings.csv`)

Open the `clips` folder. For each clip listed in `audio_ratings.csv`, listen once or twice and give two scores:

**Naturalness: does it sound like a real person speaking?**

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 5 | Excellent: sounds completely natural |
| 4 | Good: mostly natural, small oddities |
| 3 | Fair: clearly synthetic but acceptable |
| 2 | Poor: unnatural, distracting |
| 1 | Bad: very robotic or broken |

**Intelligibility: how easily can you understand every word?**

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 5 | Every word is clear |
| 4 | Almost every word is clear |
| 3 | Most words are clear, some effort needed |
| 2 | Many words are hard to make out |
| 1 | Mostly not understandable |

Pay attention to tones and pronunciation. A wrong tone that changes a word's meaning should lower the intelligibility score.

## 2. Translations (`translation_ratings.csv`, Yoruba/Igbo/Hausa only)

Each row has a short English passage (one or two sentences from a summary of a Nigerian document) and a machine translation of it. Give two scores:

**Adequacy: how much of the English meaning is kept?** 5 = all of it, 4 = most, 3 = much, 2 = little, 1 = none or wrong meaning.

**Fluency: is it natural, correct language?** 5 = flawless, 4 = good, 3 = understandable but awkward, 2 = poor, 1 = not the language / incomprehensible.

Do not let the score for one affect the other: a translation can be fluent but wrong, or accurate but awkward.

## When you're done

Save both files with your initials added to the name, e.g. `audio_ratings_AO.csv` and `translation_ratings_AO.csv`, and send them back. Please rate on your own without discussing clips with other raters. You can add comments in the last column.
